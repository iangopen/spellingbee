2. The difficulty screen is the old design again

The tier selection returns to the old live screen: eight elongated hexagon bars in one stacked column, the sage, olive, amber, burnt-orange, honey and rust ramp, "Best" scores on the right, the Practice mode and Hide definition chips with the hint line, and a rim that thickens on focus (an outline would be clipped by the hexagon). It is eight tier buttons, one per row, never a letter board.

  • Restyled with the hybrid's palette: the bar tints now come from the hybrid's tier tokens over the hybrid's panel colour, and the focused rim uses the hybrid's focus colour with its soft honey glow. Expert is the honey accent itself and Master is ember, as before.
  • One change to the layout: the title, tagline, chips and hint sit on one lit panel instead of bare background, because text may not sit on the bare honeycomb at its peak (the same rule every other screen follows).
  • At "more" the bars take slightly irregular hexagon points and tiny alternating tilts. At "light" they are exactly the old shape.

3. What "light" and "more" change pick pending

  • Light: softly wobbly corners on panels, buttons and fields; one thin hand-drawn outline round each panel; the title, headings and badges in a hand-lettered face with a drawn underline; one tilted sticker on the home and results cards; paper grain on the page behind the panels only; hard marker-style shadows on buttons instead of gloss; a calmer honeycomb glow; the bee redrawn with a slightly wobbly line.
  • More: all of light, plus a double hand-drawn outline, grain inside the panels (very faint), washi tape on three cards, panels, tier bars and avatar tokens tilted by up to three degrees, an irregular hexagon on the tier bars, wobbly icon strokes, and a sketched bee and avatars: wobbly double outline with the colour fill nudged off the line.
  • Unchanged in both: the correct-answer light-up, the bell and shake, the screen transitions, the shimmering honeycomb (calmer), the visible focus moat and ring, 44px targets, the single global reduced-motion block.

4. The answer moments

Correct: the field lights up green, a tick appears, the placard glows and +14 floats up. Missed: the bell rings, the field gives one short shake, the edge turns red and the word is shown. Both survive at every strength.

5. Checks

  • Contrast over the glow and the texture: every text element, control edge and focus ring against the worst pixel behind it, honeycomb at its peak, grain on, both themes, both widths, on all four screens. Off, light and more each have 0 failing: off, light, more.
  • Tier bar focus: a clipped hexagon cannot show an outline, so the rim thickens and takes the focus colour; it is measured against the band inside it and the one outside it, at every strength, both themes and both widths: 12/12 configurations pass, lowest 7.35:1. The same file folds that and the contrast tables into one line, 0 failures (680 contrast pairs and 12 focus configurations): checks.txt. At "more" the focused bar straightens out of its tilt so the ring stays crisp.
  • Reduced motion: the shimmer is removed and the still page is pixel-identical to the static page at every strength, screen, width and theme. The wobbles, tilts and textures are static by nature; nothing new animates: checks.txt.
  • Fonts: the hand face appears only on the title, headings and badges and never on a digit, input, button, label or instruction; the readable face is confirmed on the words, answers, instructions and buttons: checks.txt.
  • Distinct from NYT's look: 0 lemon-yellow pixels, 0 grey tiles, honeycomb is background only, and the only hexagon-shaped content anywhere is the stack of eight tier bars (one column, never a board): checks.txt.
